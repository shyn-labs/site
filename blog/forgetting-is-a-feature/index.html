entry no. 006

forgetting is a feature

3 September 2026 · the shyn daybook

Every memory product pitches total recall. Nobody pitches the other half: a register that can only grow is not a memory, it is a file someone keeps on you. You happen to be the someone, which helps, but the property that makes an archive of your days tolerable is not that it remembers everything. It is that you can make it stop.

So shyn forgets three ways, and each one was designed, not conceded.

Scheduled forgetting

Screen captures age out on a rolling window — thirty days by default, yours to change. Ambient context loses value fast: the half-written email shyn glimpsed in April will not answer any question you ask in July, and keeping it forever is all liability and no recall. Meetings run on the opposite default. Decisions age well, so transcripts are kept until you say otherwise. The asymmetry is the design: context expires, commitments persist.

Deliberate forgetting

The forget tool deletes by document, by source, or by time range — an afternoon, a week, a whole month of your register, gone on request. Two properties matter. It always asks before it acts; your AI cannot quietly decide your Tuesday should disappear. And it is a hard delete, not a hidden flag: the rows are purged, the vectors are swept, and a document that was mid-embedding when you forgot it stays forgotten — we fixed the race where the indexer could resurrect a deleted memory seconds after the funeral.

Constitutional forgetting

Some things are erased before you could even ask. Meeting audio is destroyed the moment the transcript lands — that story is entry no. 004. The recording never becomes a thing you have to remember to delete.

The cost, honestly

Forgetting is real loss. A purged month answers no questions, ever, and the rolling window will occasionally eat something you wanted on day thirty-one. We could soften this — trash cans, soft deletes, a ninety-day undo. We decided not to, because every undo is a copy, and every copy is a place your memory lives that you no longer control. A delete that costs nothing deletes nothing.

Total recall is a pitch. A memory you can edit is a possession. The second one is the product.
