entry no. 008

the far side has to speak

8 September 2026 · the shyn daybook

Every recorder asks permission once and then trusts itself forever. You grant microphone access in September and the thing holds standing consent until you uninstall it. shyn has that permission too. It is not what decides whether a recording happens.

What decides is the audio. A meeting is admitted only when the far side speaks.

A call has two voices

shyn records two channels: your microphone, and the audio your Mac plays. Detection starts on device activity, because starting is cheap and reversible — what begins is a pre-roll, kept only if it earns its place. Earning it requires voice on the system channel. Someone who is not you, arriving through your speakers.

Your own voice is optional, and that asymmetry is deliberate. Sitting muted through a briefing is a real meeting, and one of the ones you most want kept; requiring your microphone to have voiced would discard exactly those. But nothing commits without the far side. Music does not. A video does not. You, thinking out loud, does not. What survives at the end is the transcript alone — the audio is destroyed the moment the words land, which is entry no. 004.

What that refuses

We got this wrong once, in a way worth naming. For two commits the rule counted a conferencing-capable app holding either input or output audio. A video playing in a browser satisfied it completely: far-side voice from the video, a browser holding the output, no microphone involved at all. That is not a lenient gate. That is a recorder pointed at someone who is not in a meeting.

The fix was to stop reading the output stream. Only a held input counts now. And the theory that justified reading output — that a muted listener releases the microphone — turned out to be false anyway: Chrome keeps its input stream open straight through a muted call. The check bought nothing and cost the one thing the gate exists to prevent.

The cost, honestly

A room has no far side. Sit four people around a table and every voice arrives on the single microphone, the system channel stays silent, and the gate can never commit. No amount of tuning fixes that, because loosening the rule is precisely how you end up recording someone's music. So the honest answer is a switch rather than a cleverer guess:

shyn meeting start day 1 keynote
shyn meeting stop

Asking is the verification. Nothing has to be inferred from the audio when you said so out loud.

The room costs you something else as well. With one channel there is no far side to label, so shyn stops attributing lines instead of guessing: the transcript arrives unprefixed, with a note saying the speakers could not be told apart. A confident you said this that is wrong does more damage than an honest silence about who spoke.

Permission is granted once. Consent has to be re-earned every forty seconds.
