entry no. 005

a model that never leaves the room

24 July 2026 · the shyn daybook

shyn captures on your Mac and indexes on your Mac. But most people then hand the last step — the asking — to a frontier model in someone's datacenter. That is a fine trade: only the few snippets relevant to your question ever leave, for a model worth consulting. It is also, strictly, optional. shyn speaks MCP, and MCP does not care where the model lives.

So we closed the loop: Qwen3 14B running on Apple Silicon via MLX, Open WebUI in front of it, and mcpo bridging shyn's MCP socket into something Open WebUI can call:

uvx mcpo --port 8600 -- "$HOME/Library/Application Support/shyn/bin/shyn-mcp"
# then add http://localhost:8600 as a tool server in Open WebUI

Ask it what you promised a client, and the answer comes from your own meetings, ranked by your own index, spoken by a model whose weights are on your own disk. The wifi can be off. Nothing about the question, the memory, or the answer touches a network.

What we found, honestly

  • Retrieval quality is identical everywhere. The ranking — keyword plus semantic, fused — runs inside shyn's daemon, not the model. A 14B model and a frontier model receive exactly the same memories. What varies is the driver, not the archive.
  • Small models are fine clerks and poor librarians. Say “search my memory for the redwood promise” and a 14B model executes beautifully. Ask a vague question and hope it decides to search on its own — that judgment is where the frontier models earn their fees.
  • Thinking modes need reining in. Qwen3 will happily spend its whole token budget deliberating about your question instead of answering it. /no_think is your friend.
  • Name the tool. AI clients increasingly ship their own memory features. An unqualified “check my memory” can be intercepted by the client's built-in notebook; “check shyn” goes where you meant.
  • Size for your RAM with tool use in mind. A model whose weights nearly fill your unified memory will run fine in chat and die the moment tool schemas and context arrive. On a 24GB machine, the 14B was the honest choice over the 30B — a smaller model that works beats a bigger one that crashes.

Why this matters more than a benchmark

Local-first capture with cloud-only recall is half a promise. Some questions you would only ask something that provably cannot repeat them — and some people's threat models rule out the datacenter entirely. The point is not that a 14B model matches a frontier one. It is that the choice is yours, per question, forever: the register stays put, and you decide who gets to read from it.
